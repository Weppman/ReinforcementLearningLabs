Section 2.2:
4) Q-learning learns an aggressive policy that hugs the cliff edge because its update uses max_a Q(S',a), assuming optimal future behavior. SARSA learns a safer policy further from the cliff because its update uses Q(S',A') where A' is the actual ε-greedy action, so it accounts for the risk of random exploration causing a cliff fall.

5) With ε=0 evaluation, Q-learning performs better (shorter path, higher reward) because it learned the optimal cliff-hugging route. SARSA performs worse (longer path, lower reward) because it learned a conservative route that was only optimal under exploration noise.

6) With ε=0 training, both algorithms struggle due to lack of exploration. Q-learning may still learn a reasonable policy through its off-policy max operator but can get stuck in local optima. SARSA performs worse since it's on-policy and can only learn from actions it actually takes - it will likely converge to the first path it finds and never explore better alternatives.
